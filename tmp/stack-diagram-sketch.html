Desktop flow

Two devices of equal weight under the hub. Inside the laptop, herdr and Zed as equal-height panels you swipe between; inside herdr, a workflow and the cross review it spawned.

What changed

  • Sandboxes hold one chip each. The skill name moved entirely to the container, so each sandbox is now "what it's running" plus "who's running it" on a single line. Side effect worth having: the review sandbox is visibly lighter than the workflow one.
  • The cross-family edge is vertical and centred between the two sandboxes, which cost about forty pixels of height and is the only arrow in the figure that isn't straight-and-parallel to something else.
  • Phone and MacBook are the same height, so the top level reads as two devices of equal standing rather than a main one and an accessory. The phone's contents are centred in that space.
  • The MacBook border is solid.

One thing to watch

  • Dropping the cross-family review text leaves that arrow as the only unexplained relationship in the figure — a reader sees one sandbox pointing at another and has to get the meaning from the prose. If it turns out to need a label, the fix that answers your objection is a pill sitting on the arrow, the way ssh does, rather than floating text beside it.

Carried forward to the mobile pass

  • Side-by-side panels break first. Stacking herdr above Zed loses the swipe metaphor, so it's worth deciding whether mobile keeps Zed at all — the mobile story in the post is herdr and the GitHub app.
  • Equal-height devices won't survive either; stacked, the phone sits above the laptop and the ssh edge turns vertical, which reads better than it does here.
  • The hub bar is the one element that carries across both layouts unchanged, which still makes it the anchor for progressive highlighting.
